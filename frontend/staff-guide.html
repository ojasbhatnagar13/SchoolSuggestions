For teachers

How to review
suggestions.

A five minute read

Signing in

Open the staff page and sign in with your school Google account. If it says "Staff access required", your account hasn't been added yet. Ask Ojas Bhatnagar to add it.

1. Look at the red box first

If a student has written about bullying, self-harm, abuse or feeling unsafe, it appears in a red box at the top of the page instead of the normal list. The tab title also changes so it's hard to miss.

Pass it to the Head of Pastoral Care the same day. If anyone may be in danger, follow the safeguarding procedure straight away. The student is anonymous and can't be contacted from here, so act on what they wrote: the grade, the place, the time.

Then press "mark handled". If it is clearly just a suggestion (for example "start an anti-bullying club"), press "Not a concern" to move it to the normal list. When in doubt, treat it as a concern.

2. Decide on each suggestion

New suggestions are shown first. Each one has the AI's verdict and a short reason. The AI only advises. Nothing reaches students until you choose one of these:

  • Approve for voting: it goes on the student site and students can vote for it. Use this when the school wants to know how many students care.
  • Go ahead, no vote: the school will just do it. Good for things that obviously need fixing, like a broken fan.
  • Reject: it isn't going forward. You'll be asked if you want to give a reason (see below).
  • Spam: nonsense, jokes or abuse. The student isn't shown it on their list.

3. Read the warnings

Some suggestions carry coloured warnings from the AI:

  • Rude wording: a real point said rudely. Reword it rather than rejecting it.
  • Names someone: mentions a teacher or student by name. Reword it before it goes public.
  • Sarcastic: reword it into a plain request.
  • No suggestion: describes a problem but asks for nothing. Reword it into a request, or reject it.
  • Several requests: asks for more than one thing.
  • Hidden concern: a normal suggestion with something worrying inside it. Read it carefully.
  • Waiting for AI check: the AI was busy. It is checked automatically within a few minutes, but you can decide on it before then.

4. Reword when the suggestion is good but the words aren't

Press "Reword". You can edit the text yourself or press "Ask AI to rephrase" for a draft. Check the draft still says what the student meant, then press "Save wording". Students only ever see the new wording. You can put the original back with "Restore the original".

5. Give a reason when you reject

When you reject a suggestion, a box asks if you want to tell the student why. It's optional, but one sentence helps a lot, for example "The school already runs this on Fridays." The student sees it on their My suggestions page. They never see who wrote it.

6. Change your mind, or mark it Done

Every suggestion has a "Move to" menu, so any decision can be changed later. When the school has actually done something, move it to Actioned. Students then see it marked as Done, which is the best proof that the box works.

What stays private

  • You never see which student sent a suggestion.
  • Nobody, staff included, can see which teacher approved, rejected or changed a suggestion.
  • Students never see vote counts, so nothing wins just because it already looks popular.

Further down the staff page

The guidelines are the rules the AI checks against, in plain English. If the AI keeps getting something wrong, edit them and press save. The very next suggestion uses the new version.

Site feedback is what students say about the site itself. Mark each one handled once it has been read.
