How it works

The AI sorts.
People decide.

A paper suggestion box loses suggestions, collects the same one twenty times, and never answers. This one checks, sorts and replies in seconds, and leaves every real decision to staff.

The short version

What this is,
in plain words.

A two minute read for students and teachers

Sending a suggestion

This is a place to tell the school what would make DPSI better. You sign in with your school account, write your suggestion, and send it. You can also say how it would help. Staff never see who sent it.

What happens next

A computer program reads it first, in a few seconds. It checks it against the school's guidelines, spots messages that aren't real suggestions, and notices if someone has already suggested the same thing. It doesn't make the final decision. If your suggestion breaks a guideline, you're told which one straight away.

Then a teacher reads it. They can put it up for voting, let it go ahead without a vote if it simply needs doing (like fixing something broken), or decide not to take it forward. Nothing is shown to other students until a teacher has approved it.

Voting

Everyone can vote for the suggestions they want most. You get one vote per suggestion, and you can take it back any time. Vote counts are hidden, so a suggestion doesn't win just because it already looks popular. When the school acts on one, it's marked Done.

Following your own

On the My suggestions page you can see where each of yours stands. When a teacher decides on one, you'll see a notice at the top of the site. If you switch on "Complete anonymity" when sending, it isn't linked to you at all, so you won't get updates and it won't show up in your list.

What it's not for

Please don't use it for personal problems. If you're being bullied, feel unsafe or are going through something hard, talk to a teacher or a counsellor in person, so someone can actually help you.

For teachers

Everything is on the staff page. Suggestions waiting for a decision are shown first, with the program's opinion next to each one. You make every decision and can overturn anything it did. If a student ever reports something worrying, it appears in a red box at the top of the page and is never shown to other students.

Who does what

What the AI does,
and what it never does.

What the AI checks

  • Whether it breaks a school guideline, with the guideline quoted back to you
  • Whether it's a real suggestion at all, or spam, abuse, or an attempt to trick the system
  • Whether the same suggestion has already been made, however it's worded
  • A category and a one-line summary, so staff can read the queue quickly

What only people decide

  • Whether a suggestion is approved, and whether it needs a vote
  • Overturning any automatic decision the AI got wrong
  • What the school actually acts on, and when
  • Anything that needs judgement the guidelines don't cover

Questions

Before you send one.

Is it really anonymous?

From staff and other students, yes. Your suggestion is saved with your account so that you can follow what happens to it, but the staff page has no way to show who sent anything, and nobody else can see your list. The only people who could look it up are the system's administrators, with direct access to the database, and the school decides who that is. If you want nothing at all to link a suggestion to you, switch on "Complete anonymity" when you send it. You just won't get any updates about it, so you'd check the Vote page yourself.

How do I know what happened to my suggestion?

Sign in and open the My suggestions page. It shows where each one stands. When staff approve one, let it go ahead, mark it done or decide against it, you'll see a notice at the top of the site, on any device.

Can I report bullying or a problem here?

Please don't. Talk to a teacher or a school counsellor in person. This box is anonymous, so nobody can reply to you, check you're okay, or help you through it. If something is sent here anyway, staff see it straight away and pass it to the pastoral care team.

Why was my suggestion turned down?

Either it broke one of the guidelines above, or it didn't read as a real suggestion. You'll have been told which. If the AI got it wrong, reword it or speak to a teacher. Staff see everything the AI turns down and can overturn it.

Why can't I see my suggestion yet?

Nothing appears on the Vote page until a member of staff approves it. That's deliberate: every suggestion other students see has been read by a person first.

It said my suggestion already exists.

Someone suggested the same thing, possibly in different words. Rather than filing it twice, you're shown the original, which you can vote for once it's on the Vote page.

Why do I have to sign in?

So only students can send suggestions and see the list, and so nobody can flood the box by opening a private window or switching network. Google also proves the account is real.

Is there a limit on how many I can send?

Five an hour and twenty a day for each student, to stop spam. If you reach it, wait a while and try again. It resets on its own.
